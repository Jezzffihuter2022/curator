# Authenticating, Dating and Condition-Grading Vintage Enamel + Rhinestone Brooches (Remote/Online Purchase)

Scope: luxury houses and quality US/European costume makers, pre-c.1990, gold-plated base metal or gold-plated sterling (vermeil). The buyer is in China and buying remotely.

Source-quality note: most of the accessible material comes from collector or dealer sites (Morning Glory Antiques, Costume Jewelry Collectors Int'l, Antique Trader, Kovels, VFG forum) and from retail blogs. No primary corporate archive was reachable. Mark date ranges vary between references by a few years, and the references themselves call their dates approximate. Treat every date below as a range for cross-checking, not as proof. I did not use Illusion Jewels: the Boucher page URL I tried returned 404.

## 1. Signature/mark verification and dating for key makers

### Takeaway
Marks give an approximate production window (for example Trifari crown without © is c.1937–1955 and crown with © is c.1955–1969; for many US makers the © appears after 1955), but marks overlap and were reused. Date a piece by mark + construction + design patent/advertising together, never by mark alone. Fakers deliberately strike famous marks onto designs the house never made, so check the design against documented examples as well as the mark.

### Cited Findings
**General US rule (©):**
- For Boucher, Coro and Eisenberg, Morning Glory notes "copyright symbol used after 1955". Hobé's © use began in 1958. — [Morning Glory A–D](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/a-through-d/); [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)
- Trifari added © to its crown mark around 1955. One account ties the change to a court win against a copier that strengthened design-copyright protection. — [Search summary of collector sources incl. collectingcostumejewelry101.com](https://collectingcostumejewelry101.com/?p=2329); [Kovels](https://kovels.com/blog/costume-jewelry)

**Trifari:**
- "TRIFARI PAT. PEND." from the early 1930s. Crown-over-T mark c.1937–1955. Crown + © c.1955–1969. Crown dropped in the 1970s. "Trifari TM" in the late 1980s. — [collectingcostumejewelry101.com](https://collectingcostumejewelry101.com/?p=2329); [Kovels – Trifari](https://kovels.com/blog/costume-jewelry); [1stDibs Q&A](https://www.1stdibs.com/answers/how-to-identify-trifari-jewelry/)
- Caveat: collectors report ads dating some pieces to after 1955 that are signed without ©. Published dates are "best guess" from design patents, advertising and the signature/construction. — [collectingcostumejewelry101.com](https://collectingcostumejewelry101.com/?p=2329)
- The earlier "KTF" mark came before "Trifari". Genuine Trifari figurals often carry a patent number or "PAT PEND"; contemporary copies (e.g. Peter & Helen copies of Trifari) lacked the signature, the patent mark and Trifari's signed fur-clip mechanism. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)
- WWII: Trifari switched to sterling silver during the war. According to Cooper Hewitt this tripled prices without hurting sales, and after the war consumers preferred sterling even though Trifari wanted to return to cheaper metal. — [Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2018/07/03/a-celebration-of-unity-sweetheart-jewelry-from-the-world-war-ii-era/)

**Coro / Corocraft:**
- "CORO" (Cohn & Rosenberger) 1901–1979, incorporated 1910, © after 1955. "CORO STERLING" is an early mark. "CORO Sterling Craft" c.1940. "COROCRAFT" c.1940. "COROCRAFT sterling Pegasus mark" c.1942–1947. "COROCRAFT Pegasus mark" c.1945. "CORO DUETTE" c.1935. "FRANCOIS" (a Coro trade name) 1938–c.1960. — [Morning Glory A–D](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/a-through-d/); [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)
- Coro designs were reissued in the 1960s under the Vendome mark, so the same design can carry a later mark. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)

**Marcel Boucher:**
- "MB" Phrygian-cap mark 1937–1949 (Boucher worked for Mazer, then founded his own firm in 1937). "MARBOUX" 1938–1960s (Morning Glory marks this with a question mark). "BOUCHER" 1937–1971, © after 1955. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/); [Morning Glory A–D](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/a-through-d/)
- Inventory numbers: Wikipedia, citing Joanne Dubbs Ball (1990), gives inventory numbers 2300–2350 as 1945. I could not verify a full number-to-year table. — [Wikipedia – Marcel Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Boucher)
- Some Boucher pieces are unsigned, so an unsigned piece is not automatically a fake. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)

**Eisenberg:**
- "EISENBERG ORIGINAL" 1935–1945 ("Original" was first a clothing-line term and was applied to jewelry c.1935–38). "EISENBERG STERLING" 1943–1948. Script "E" 1942–1945. Block "EISENBERG" 1945–1958, © after 1955. Unsigned 1958–1970. "EISENBERG ICE" + © 1970 to closure (the Ice name also appears on 1940s accessories). — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)
- CJCI places script "E" c.1942–1945 and notes it may have been used later where space was limited. Collectors note Eisenberg marks overlap and all dates are approximate. — [CJCI "E" marks](https://www.costumejewelrycollectors.com/vintage-costume-jewelry-research/costume-jewelry-marks/costume-jewelry-history-jewelry-signatures-e/)
- Most "Eisenberg Original" pieces sold by veteran dealers are authentic. Be cautious of coloured-rhinestone pieces or rare figurals marked "Eisenberg Original" at too-good-to-be-true prices. "The Eisenberg Project" (Sutton) is recommended for learning marks. — [Antique Trader – Collecting Eisenberg](https://www.antiquetrader.com/collectibles/eisenberg-costume-jewelry)

**Mazer / Jomaz:**
- "MAZER Bros" (Joseph & Louis Mazer) 1926–1951; Matt Mazer's dates are 1921–1948. "JOMAZ" (Joseph Mazer) 1946–1981; Matt Mazer's dates are 1947–1980. "ADOLFO for MAZER" c.1980. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)

**Hattie Carnegie:**
- "HC" mark c.1939 to the late 1940s. "Hattie CARNEGIE" c.1950–1979. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)

**Hobé, Kramer, Givenchy:**
- Hobé operated in the US 1927–1992. A "HOBE Sterling" mark exists (undated on this page). Hobé © from 1958. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)
- "KRAMER" 1943–1980 (Louis Kramer, NY). Some Julio pieces are "exactly like certain Hobe and Dior by Kramer jewelry", so lookalikes exist. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)
- "GIVENCHY" 1952–present. — [Morning Glory E–M](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/e-through-m/)

**Weiss:**
- D&E (Juliana) made for Weiss, Sarah Coventry and Avon, so Weiss-style unsigned pieces need research. — [Collectors Weekly thread](https://collectorsweekly.com/stories/49301-unsigned-weiss?in=all)
- Weiss never made the red/yellow speckled-enamel pear brooches often attributed to it; that design is a DuJay original. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)

**Christian Dior (incl. Germany / Henkel & Grosse):**
- "DIOR" 1948–present. A "CHRISTIAN DIOR BOUTIQUE … GERMANY" mark exists. Mitchell Maer made Dior jewelry from 1952, and Kramer and Grosse also made some Dior pieces (Morning Glory spells the German firm "Grosse and Hinkle"). — [Morning Glory A–D](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/a-through-d/)
- Many Dior designs are dated. In the 1980s larger oval plaques reading "Christian Dior Boutique" were used. — [Antique Trader – Dior](https://www.antiquetrader.com/collectibles/christian-dior-jewelry)
- Dealer listings show Henkel & Grosse marks of the form "19 – GROSSE – 67 – GERMANY" and "19 GROSSE 70 GERMANY", with the year split around GROSSE, and dealers date pieces accordingly (e.g. 1959, 1962, 1963, 1964, 1967). — [Proantic 1967 listing](https://proantic.com/en/1400427-brooch-henkel-amp-grosse-for-christian-dior-1967.html); [1stDibs H&G for Dior brooches](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/henkel-and-grosse-for-christian-dior/jewelry/brooches); [1stDibs 1959 example](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/brooches/brooches/1959-christian-dior-henkel-grosse-red-green-crystal-brooch/id-v_7976832/). FLAG: this year-reading is a consistent dealer convention seen across listings. I found no official decoding table.

**Chanel:**
- Morning Glory lists "CHANEL" (Coco Chanel, undated) and a separate unrelated "CHANEL" by Chanel Novelty Co., NYC, c.1940, taken over by Reinad in 1941. That is a US costume maker, not the Paris house, and buyers should know about it. — [Morning Glory A–D](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/jewelry-marks-and-dates/a-through-d/)
- Era timeline from a commercial jeweler's guide (UNVERIFIED against Chanel records):
  - after 1954: plain stamped "CHANEL"
  - after 1971: © left and ® right of CHANEL
  - from 1981: a four-digit year replaces the country at the bottom of the plaque
  - from 1984: season numbers either side of CC
  - Victoire de Castellane-era pieces (1986–1992): oval signature tag
  - 1990s onward: season codes such as "94A"
  — [Opulent Jewelers](https://www.opulentjewelers.com/blog/how-to-spot-fake-chanel-jewelry/); see also [1stDibs Q&A](https://www.1stdibs.com/answers/how-to-authenticate-chanel-brooch) and [TheRealReal](https://realstyle.therealreal.com/?p=9017). Sellers stress that stamps alone are not proof and that construction and materials must agree.

### Inferences
- A mark that is anachronistic for the construction is a red flag for a fake or a marriage. Examples: a © on a piece claimed as 1940s; a "Pat. Pend." crown Trifari with modern findings; a "Chanel" plaque with a year format inconsistent with its claimed decade.
- For Trifari, Coro, Boucher and Eisenberg figurals, searching the design patent on Google Patents (patents.google.com, "USD" + number) to confirm the design matches the drawing is a strong check where a patent number is stamped. I did not run specific patent lookups.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found for a full Boucher inventory-number-to-year table (Wikipedia gives only one range; Dubbs Ball 1990 is the cited reference).
- No verified YSL vintage mark chronology was found.
- No primary/official Chanel or Dior mark chronology was found. The Chanel timeline above is commercial and unverified.
- Trifari "Trifari Sterling" exact date window and the Alfred Philippe attribution were not independently verified beyond collector summaries.
- I found no reliable published source documenting the claim that counterfeit vintage Chanel/Dior brooches are common in Asian markets. It is plausible, but unsourced here.

## 2. Spotting reproductions, fakes, marriages and repairs

### Takeaway
Fakes target the most coveted signed designs (Trifari/Coro jelly bellies, Eisenberg, the Boucher grasshopper and gryphon, the Staret torch). They give themselves away through poorly struck signatures, a famous name on a design the house never made, thin or overly bright plating, light pot metal, cheap findings, fewer or plastic stones, and poor enamel. Hybrids or marriages (e.g. a brooch refitted with Lucite to make a "jelly belly") and anachronistic findings are the other main traps. No single feature proves a fake.

### Cited Findings
- Fakes are newly made, often with a poorly struck signature or a famous name on a design the company never produced. Period copies are usually unsigned, genuinely vintage and worth less. Fakes target Trifari and Coro jelly bellies, Eisenberg, the Boucher grasshopper and the Staret torch. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)
- Boucher gryphon reproduction tells: genuine pieces have "thick, shiny rhodium plating"; the reproduction was pot metal, felt light, had cheaper findings and enamel that looked off. Copies generally use "cheaper components, fewer stones, and lower quality enameling". Be wary of blurry photos and listings with no photo of the back. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)
- Jelly belly checks:
  - Originals from the 1940s have very fine detailing front and back, with the Lucite often fastened by a set-screw.
  - New ones can be oversized, with bright plating and a cruder look.
  - Be wary of jelly bellies signed Boucher or Hobé, since those firms did not make a Lucite line.
  - "Hybrids" are brooches with the centre stone removed and replaced by Lucite.
  - Use a 10x or stronger loupe.
  - It takes more than one questionable feature to call a piece fake.
  — [BellaOnline](https://www.bellaonline.com/ArticlesP/art42972.asp); [Antique Trader – Jelly Belly](https://www.antiquetrader.com/collectibles/jelly-belly-jewelry) (quotes surfaced via search summary. Which phrase comes from which of these two pages was not individually confirmed.)
- Findings as alteration evidence: a modern catch on a piece claimed as older may be a replacement, and hardware may show the brooch has been "altered, repaired or re-housed". — [AC Silver – Dating Brooches by Clasp](https://blog.acsilver.co.uk/?p=15018); [Navette Jewellery](https://navettejewellery.org/2018/05/28/dating-brooches-clasps-part-1/)
- Fake prongs (cast decorative prongs with stones actually glued) appear on some pieces and complicate attribution. — [Collectors Weekly Weiss thread](https://www.collectorsweekly.com/stories/242034-marked-weissco-crystal-brooch-mystery)

### Inferences
- Marriage and repair signs to look for in photos:
  - lead or tin solder blobs (grey, dull) near the pin hinge or catch
  - a catch or hinge in a different metal colour or plating tone from the back plate
  - glue residue around stones
  - stones of a slightly different colour, size or cut from their neighbours
  - a replating sheen that is uniform over areas that should show wear
  These are standard collector heuristics. I did not find one authoritative source listing them all.
- Request a photo of the full back under raking light. Struck marks on genuine pieces are typically crisp and sit in a cartouche or plaque consistent with the brand's era.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found with specific counterfeit-mark tells (font, depth) for Eisenberg, Chanel or Dior.
- No source found specific to Asian-market counterfeit production of vintage-style Chanel/Dior brooches.
- A page titled "How to Spot Fake Weiss Jewelry" (vintagecostumejewels.com) was referenced in forums but I could not read it.

## 3. Judging workmanship (stone setting, enamel type, plating, construction)

### Takeaway
Quality markers are hand-set, prong/claw-set stones, crisp casting, heavy plating (rhodium on Boucher; thick gold plating) and fine detailing on the back as well as the front. Glued settings and resin "cold enamel" are typical of most costume jewelry and are not by themselves signs of a fake. Fired (vitreous) enamel is rare on base-metal costume pieces and very hard to repair.

### Cited Findings
- Claw/prong-set jewelry is generally higher quality than adhesive-set. Glued stones fall out as the glue shrinks with age, and many glues are water-soluble, so water or alcohol can loosen stones. — [Quick Jewelry Repairs – stone replacement](https://quickjewelryrepairs.com/articles/costume-jewelry-stone-replacement/); [Kovels – Caring for Rhinestones](https://kovels.com/kovels-komments/tips-on-care-and-repairs/caring-for-rhinestones.html/amp)
- Quality stones and hand-setting mark genuine high-end work (e.g. the "breathtakingly handset" stones in a Trifari piece). — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)
- Fired enamel is glass fused in a kiln. Cold enamel is a resin, typically a two-part epoxy, cured at room temperature or with mild heat. Cold enamel looks waxier or more plastic, and under magnification it shows more scratches; fired enamel has a deep glassy shine. Fired glass chips in sharp, glassy fractures, while resin flakes or peels. — [Skyjems encyclopedia – cold enamelling](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-cold-enamelling); [Mervis Diamond](https://www.mervisdiamond.com/blogs/blog/enamel-jewelry-repair-guide-2026) (retail blog). Do not use heat tests on vintage pieces.
- The GIA overview of enamel techniques (cloisonné, champlevé, guilloché, etc.) is a background reference. — [GIA 4Cs blog](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/jewelry-enameling-techniques/)
- Wartime vermeil was sterling coated with a very thin gold layer to look like gold. — [VFG forum Vintage Jewelry Workshop IV](https://forums.vintagefashionguild.org/threads/vintage-jewelry-workshop-part-iv.35702/)

### Inferences
- On mid-century US costume brooches, "enamel" is most often cold-painted. Expect this, and judge quality by crisp colour edges, even coverage and no brush marks, not by whether the enamel is vitreous. This is a widely held collector understanding but was not sourced explicitly here.
- On sterling/vermeil pieces, worn high points showing whitish-grey metal suggest silver under the gold; yellow-brass or pinkish-copper showing through suggests a brass/copper-alloy base. This is a common heuristic. I found no citable source.
- Sterling vs plated brass, testing: a "STERLING" stamp is the main remote indicator. Weight comparison is weak evidence. Silver acid testing scratches the piece and should be done only on a hidden spot by a professional. XRF is non-destructive and the preferred check on valuable pieces. I found no citable source in this pass, so all of this is flagged unsourced.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found distinguishing Swarovski/Austrian crystal from Czech glass or acrylic in costume jewelry (refractive brilliance, mould seams, warmth/weight of acrylic). These distinctions are generally known in the trade but were unsourced here.
- No source on guilloché specifically in vintage costume or luxury-house brooches.
- No sourced explanation of XRF or acid-testing protocols.

## 4. Condition issues affecting value and wearability, plus remote-buying and care

### Takeaway
The main value-killers are:
- dead (foil-damaged) stones, which cannot be restored
- missing or replaced stones
- enamel loss or touch-ups
- plating wear through to base metal
- green verdigris on copper-alloy bases
- metal fatigue, solder repairs and replaced findings at the pin, hinge and catch

Remote buyers should demand a photo of the back, close-ups of the mark, findings and stones, and photos under daylight. Afterwards, store pieces dry and separated and clean them only by dry or minimal methods.

### Cited Findings
**Stones:**
- "Dead" stones are foil-backed rhinestones whose foil has been damaged, usually by moisture, turning them dark, dull and cloudy. Nothing can restore them and they cannot be cleaned, so they must be replaced. — [Kovels – Caring for Rhinestones](https://kovels.com/kovels-komments/tips-on-care-and-repairs/caring-for-rhinestones.html/amp); [eBay community thread](https://community.ebay.com/t5/Jewelry-Gemstones-Board-Group/Restoring-a-piece-maybe-dark-or-missing-rhinestones-Anyone-here/m-p/28688463)
- Replacement:
  - Glued stones are the easier case: remove with acetone and a swab. Note that some glues attack foil backing.
  - Prong-set stones are harder: on a multi-stone piece, breaking a prong can require removing all the other stones before soldering.
  - Source old replacement stones from dealers to match.
  — [Quick Jewelry Repairs](https://quickjewelryrepairs.com/articles/costume-jewelry-stone-replacement/); [Ganoksin](https://orchid.ganoksin.com/t/using-stones-in-costume-jewelry/37059)

**Enamel:**
- Cold enamel can be touched up with resin, but matching aged or yellowed colour is hard. Fired enamel needs re-firing by a specialist. For collector value, use conservative repair and disclose it. — [Skyjems](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-cold-enamelling); [Mervis Diamond](https://www.mervisdiamond.com/blogs/blog/enamel-jewelry-repair-guide-2026)
- Nail polish or model paint are common cosmetic "fixes" on costume enamel, so they are what to look for as undisclosed touch-ups. — [Ganoksin forum](https://orchid.ganoksin.com/t/costume-jewelry-paint-repair/32000)

**Plating and corrosion:**
- Verdigris is a copper corrosion product that forms on copper, brass and bronze, including pieces once plated in gold or silver. It is accelerated by sweat, oils, cosmetics, perfume and hair products. Worn plating exposes the base metal. — [WireJewelry – Prevent and Remove Verdigris](https://www.wirejewelry.com/jewelry_making_tips_techniques/Prevent-and-Remove-Verdigris_250.html); [Robinsons Jewelers](https://robinsonsjewelers.com/blogs/news/how-to-clean-costume-jewelry-thats-turning-green-diy-fix)
- Conservation literature notes corrosion is most often found where an object is damaged or its plating is defective. — [Canadian Conservation Institute Note 9/1](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cci-icc/documents/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/9-1-eng.pdf) (via search summary)

**Findings and dating clues:**
- The C-clasp is predominantly 19th century, but it also appears on later pieces, so it sets only an earliest possible date. The trombone catch is European; its introduction is dated variously from the 1850s to the late 1890s, and it was popular into the 1940s (sources conflict). The roll-over safety catch became common in the 1920s–30s. A long pin stem extending past the edge is associated with older pieces. — [AC Silver](https://blog.acsilver.co.uk/?p=15018); [Navette Jewellery](https://navettejewellery.org/2018/05/28/dating-brooches-clasps-part-1/); [Skyjems – C clasp](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-c-clasp); [VFG forum](https://forums.vintagefashionguild.org/threads/vintage-jewelry-workshop-part-i.35650/post-343041). Morning Glory has a findings reference: [Jewelry Findings](https://www.morninggloryjewelry.com/articles/reference-information/jewelry-findings/)

**Cleaning and storage:**
- Clean with a toothpick, cotton swab or dry soft brush. Do not scrape or scrub, and avoid commercial cleaners, ultrasonic machines and acidic home remedies (vinegar, lemon) on plated costume pieces. Use a conservator for valuable pieces. — [WireJewelry](https://www.wirejewelry.com/jewelry_making_tips_techniques/Prevent-and-Remove-Verdigris_250.html); [Yahoo/never use jewelry cleaner on costume](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/why-never-jewelry-cleaner-polish-153724301.html); [Robinsons Jewelers](https://robinsonsjewelers.com/blogs/news/how-to-clean-costume-jewelry-thats-turning-green-diy-fix)
- Avoid getting water or alcohol near glued stones, since the glue may dissolve. — [Kovels](https://kovels.com/kovels-komments/tips-on-care-and-repairs/caring-for-rhinestones.html/amp)
- Storage:
  - Keep pieces separate, cool and dry, in sealed bags with the air removed or boxes with anti-tarnish strips.
  - Keep copper-based pieces apart, since verdigris can spread among stored pieces.
  - Isolate an affected piece in acid-free paper.
  - One affected piece suggests the storage is too humid.
  — [WireJewelry](https://wirejewelry.com/jewelry-making-blog/5388/prevent-remove-verdigris); [Robinsons Jewelers](https://robinsonsjewelers.com/blogs/news/how-to-clean-costume-jewelry-thats-turning-green-diy-fix)

**Remote buying:**
- Blurry photos and listings with no photo of the back can hide the differences between originals and copies. — [Robin Deutsch, CJCI](https://costumejewelrycollectors.com/costume-jewelry-collecting/start-or-grow-collection/the-piece-of-your-dreams-or-is-it-by-robin-deutsch/)
- Collectors routinely ask for clearer close-ups of the mark before giving an attribution. — [Collectors Weekly](https://www.collectorsweekly.com/stories/242034-marked-weissco-crystal-brooch-mystery)

### Inferences
- Photos to request:
  - front and back in daylight, plus one at an angle to show foil sparkle versus dull dead stones
  - a macro of the mark
  - macros of the hinge, catch and pin stem
  - edges and high points, to show plating wear and base-metal colour
  - each enamel area
  - a side profile to show depth and casting
  - the piece on a scale, with its dimensions
- Questions to ask the seller:
  - Any replaced, missing or reglued stones?
  - Any enamel touch-up or replating?
  - Any solder repair or replaced findings?
  - Does the pin close securely and the catch lock?
  - Any odour or green residue?
  - What is the provenance?
- Red flags in listings:
  - a famous mark at a bargain price
  - a design that does not appear in references or patents
  - a © on a "1940s" piece
  - bright, uniform plating on a "worn vintage" piece
  - plastic-looking stones
  - no photo of the back
  - stock or reused photos
  - "style of" or "inspired" wording in a branded listing
- For a buyer in China: cross-border returns are costly, so prefer platforms with buyer protection covering "not as described" (e.g. eBay Money Back Guarantee, 1stDibs or Etsy policies). Confirm return shipping terms before buying. Pay through the platform's own payment system. Expect customs and handling time. Platform policy details were not researched here and should be verified with each platform.
- Wearability: the pin stem, hinge and catch carry the mechanical load. Fatigue at the hinge rivet or a loose catch makes a brooch unsafe to wear (a risk of loss) even if it looks fine.
