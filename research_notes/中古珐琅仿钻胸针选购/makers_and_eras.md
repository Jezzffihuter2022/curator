## 1. American "golden age" makers known for enamel + rhinestone brooches, and the WWII sterling/vermeil era

### Takeaway
Within budget, the best American enamel + rhinestone sources are mid-tier figurals and florals:
- Trifari: Crown-T base-metal enamel pieces, and some 1940s sterling.
- Coro and Corocraft: sterling vermeil 1942–47, and later base metal.
- Hattie Carnegie: 1950s–60s enamel figurals.
- Weiss, Reja and Mazer: smaller pieces.

The WWII window, about 1942–47, is when many US makers switched to sterling, often gold-washed (vermeil). That is the buyer's most-preferred material. Prime pieces from that era (Boucher enamel birds, large Trifari or Mazer vermeil, Reja figurals, Philippe jelly bellies) usually list above budget. Smaller vermeil florals and duettes, and enamel-less sterling pieces, do sell under USD 250.

### Cited Findings

**WWII sterling era (vermeil)**
- Wartime metal rationing pushed Trifari from base metal to sterling. One account says this tripled prices without hurting sales, and that after the war consumers preferred sterling even though Trifari wanted to return to cheaper base metal. — [Vintage Fashion Guild forum, Vintage Jewelry Workshop pt IV](https://forums.vintagefashionguild.org/threads/vintage-jewelry-workshop-part-iv.35702/post-343560); [Cooper Hewitt](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/?p=30449)
- Dealer and collector summaries say sterling was used c.1942–1947, often with a vermeil (gold-plated) coating, and call vermeil "the most common base for affordable costume jewelry in the 1940s." — [Vintage Dancer, 1940s jewelry styles](https://vintagedancer.com/1940s/1940s-jewelry-styles/)
- Cooper Hewitt notes that wartime makers delivered "fine" costume jewelry to the middle class by pairing vermeil with faux stones. — [Cooper Hewitt, Sweetheart jewelry from WWII](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2018/07/03/a-celebration-of-unity-sweetheart-jewelry-from-the-world-war-ii-era/)
- No primary War Production Board order was found, so the exact restriction dates are not confirmed by a primary source. — same VFG / Vintage Dancer sources (secondhand)

### Inferences
- **Budget reality by maker** (tiering from the cited bands):
  - *Comfortably within budget (USD 30–200 typical):* Weiss; 1950s–70s Crown Trifari base-metal enamel + rhinestone figurals and florals; Hattie Carnegie smaller enamel figurals (around $60–250 on eBay and Etsy); later Coro (non-Craft); probably ART, Florenza, Kramer and Hollycraft (not price-checked this session).
  - *Borderline (USD 150–450):* Coro Craft and Corocraft sterling vermeil 1942–47 (1stDibs floor about $270, likely lower on eBay); Crown Trifari sterling rhinestone without enamel ($40–150 sold); Mazer vermeil sterling florals (around $445 at dealers, likely less at auction); Reja figurals.
  - *Generally above budget:* 1930s–40s Boucher enamel birds and tremblants; Philippe jelly bellies in prime condition; large Trifari or Mazer vermeil statement pieces; Reja figurals at 1stDibs retail.
- **Sterling + enamel + rhinestone under budget is most likely with:** Coro and Corocraft sterling vermeil florals, birds and duettes; Trifari sterling enamel florals or smaller figurals (not jelly bellies); smaller Reja or Mazer pieces; and less-famous WWII sterling makers. Background knowledge, not price-checked this session: Monet sterling, Pennino sterling and "Danecraft" lean silver-only. These are best hunted on eBay or Japanese and European auctions rather than 1stDibs, where retail runs about 1.5–3× eBay levels.
- **Background knowledge, not verified in this session's sources:**
  - Eisenberg Original (1940s) is mostly sterling with rhinestones and little enamel, with enamel figurals mainly from the 1970s on.
  - Nettie Rosenstein made sterling vermeil enamel figurals in the 1940s that are usually above budget.
  - Pennino and Staret are premium and rarely under $300 for enamel figurals.
  - Kenneth Jay Lane and Ciner enamel + crystal pieces from the 1960s–80s fall roughly in the $80–300 range.
  - Joseff is mostly Russian-gold-plated metal with stones and little enamel.
  - Treat all of this as hypotheses for the report writer to flag, not findings.

### Gaps
- No systematic eBay sold data (Terapeak or WorthPoint are behind paywalls). The eBay figures are snippet-level, with uncertain dates and item-to-price matching.
- Not price-checked: Eisenberg, Nettie Rosenstein, Pennino, Staret, Kramer, Schreiner, Miriam Haskell, Joseff, Castlecliff, Sandor, Florenza, ART, Napier, KJL, Ciner, Jomaz.
- No dated Trifari enamel + rhinestone figural sold comparables from 2025–26 were captured, beyond a $105 feather (metal unknown).
- No primary source on exact WPB metal-restriction dates.

## 2. European houses: which fit and which dip under budget

### Takeaway
YSL and Givenchy gold-tone pieces from the 1970s–80s, Butler & Wilson enamel and crystal figurals from the 1980s, and Christian Dior / Grosse gilt brooches from the 1960s–80s are the European candidates. Enamel-plus-crystal examples of all three can be found within budget on Catawiki and eBay.

Chanel is the main European name that is almost always above budget: Gripoix, poured glass and enamel pieces realize about $600–800 at auction. Dior/Grosse pieces are mostly gilt with pearls and crystals, and enamel is uncommon.

- **Weiss:** $20–90.
- **Crown Trifari base-metal enamel + rhinestone:** about $50–150, often around $100.
- **Trifari sterling rhinestone without enamel:** $40–150 sold.
- **Hattie Carnegie enamel figurals:** about $60–300, with retail asks up to $1,295.
- **Corocraft sterling vermeil:** $270–1,450 retail, average $569, and lower on eBay.
- **Mazer vermeil sterling:** about $445 retail.
- **Reja sterling figurals:** about $520+ retail.
- **Boucher 1940s enamel:** $575–17,500 retail.
- **YSL:** €120–170 sold on Catawiki for plain gilt, €380–400 dealer for enamel and strass.

- 1stDibs and Chairish retail asks typically run about 1.5–3× eBay and Catawiki hammer prices for the same maker. Sub-budget buying of borderline makers (Corocraft vermeil, Mazer, Reja, Hattie Carnegie) is therefore realistic on eBay, Catawiki, Yahoo Auctions Japan or Mercari Japan, and rarely on 1stDibs. This is an inference from comparing the Hattie Carnegie $845 ask average with its $299 sold average, and the Corocraft retail floor with eBay patterns.

### Gaps
- There is no clean, dated 2025–26 sold dataset for: Trifari enamel + rhinestone figurals; Boucher later (1950s–60s gilt) enamel pieces; Coro Craft sterling enamel on eBay; Dior/Grosse enamel; YSL or Givenchy enamel. WorthPoint and Terapeak are paywalled.
- Japanese-market prices (Yahoo Auctions, Mercari) were not checked. They may be favourable given JPY weakness (about 23.9 JPY per CNY).

## 4. Workmanship markers

### Takeaway
Fired (vitreous) enamel shows glassy depth. Cold enamel, which is standard in costume jewelry, looks waxy or plastic, scratches more, and chips to bright metal. Quality markers are:
- prong-set or pavé stones rather than glued ones;
- clean enamel edges inside raised cells;
- vermeil or heavy gilt with sterling marks;
- patent or design-patent numbers;
- tremblant mechanisms;
- well-made findings: safety catches, sturdy pins and duette frames.

### Cited Findings
- Fired enamel is powdered glass fused at about 750–850 °C. Cold enamel is a resin cured at room or low temperature. Cold enamel has a "slightly plastic or waxy lustre rather than the brilliant, glass-like depth" and shows more micro-abrasions, and infrared spectroscopy can confirm it. Cold versions are always significantly cheaper. — [Skyjems encyclopedia: cold enamelling](https://skyjems.ca/pages/encyclopedia-cold-enamelling); [GIA 4Cs: jewelry enameling techniques](https://4cs.gia.edu/en-us/blog/jewelry-enameling-techniques/)
- Chipped paint is common, and shiny metal visible through chips points to a painted (cold) surface. Obvious, muddy brushstrokes suggest a low-quality or fake piece. This is a heuristic from a collector blog. — [collectingcostumejewelry101.com](https://collectingcostumejewelry101.com/?p=180); [Antique Trader, identifying antique enamels](https://antiquetrader.com/collecting-101/how-to-identify-antique-enamels)
- Collectors prize original, untouched enamel. A 1stDibs Boucher listing explicitly advertises "ORIGINAL enamel". — [1stDibs early Boucher bird of paradise](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/brooches/brooches/early-marcel-boucher-enamel-bird-paradise/id-v_9847372)
- Boucher 1940s enamel pieces are mostly rhodium-plated base metal with crystal pavé. Tremblant ("en tremblant") mechanisms appear, as in the cuckoo-clock clip of 1940. — [1stDibs Boucher enamel](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/boucher-enamel-brooch)
- Trifari jelly bellies are sterling or vermeil frames with foil-backed rhinestones and a polished Lucite belly. Patents are common: an example is Alfred Spaney's Trifari design patent 129319 of 1941. — [1stDibs jelly belly](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/brooches/brooches/trfifari-jelly-belly-flower-brooch/id-v_17369/); [eBay 326005590384 (Spaney patent 129319)](https://ebay.com/itm/326005590384)

### Inferences
- **Background, not verified in this session's sources:**
  - Better makers (Trifari, Boucher, Mazer, Reja, Pennino) prong-set or pavé-set foil-backed Austrian or Czech stones. Glued-in stones are typical of cheaper pieces and of later replacements.
  - Boucher pieces carry a model number stamped on the back.
  - "Des. Pat." or "Pat. Pend." marks date pieces to around the 1940s–50s.
  - Enamel retouching (overpaint) lowers value. Check under 10× magnification for overpaint on chips.
  - On vermeil sterling, wear exposes white silver, not brass.
- Hunt for enamel + crystal with a "Sterling" mark and gold wash first. Fallback order: heavy gold-plated base metal, then rhodium base metal.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source captured on prong-set versus glued stones, closed versus open backs, or how to grade findings. These rest on collector-practice knowledge and need a citation (e.g., Illusion Jewels or Morning Glory pages) before stating as fact.

## 5. Best-value 3–5 maker and line combinations under budget

### Takeaway
Ranked for the buyer's preferences (sterling vermeil > gold-plated brass > base metal; fine work; enamel + crystal), within a USD 230–260 item ceiling:
1. **Coro Craft / Corocraft sterling vermeil enamel + rhinestone (1942–47).** Most-preferred material. Birds, florals and duettes appear with enamel. 1stDibs retail starts at $270, and eBay and Japanese auctions are likely under $250.
2. **Crown Trifari, sterling (1942–47) or quality gold-plated base metal (1950s–60s), enamel + rhinestone florals and figurals.** Excellent stone-setting and finishing, and wide supply. Sterling rhinestone pieces sold at $40–150; an enamel + rhinestone feather sold at $105. Avoid prime Philippe jelly bellies ($650–850+).
3. **Hattie Carnegie enamel + rhinestone figurals (1950s–60s, gold-plated).** Distinctive, well-enamelled figurals. Sold average about $299 per a 1stDibs figure that needs re-verification, and $60–120 on Etsy and eBay.
4. **Mazer or Reja smaller sterling vermeil pieces (1940s).** Fine 1940s craftsmanship at the edge of budget. Retail asks are $445–530, so target auction or eBay sales under $250.
5. **European alternative: YSL or Butler & Wilson enamel + strass pieces (1970s–80s, gilt metal).** Brand prestige within budget: Catawiki sold €120–170 for gilt YSL, B&W enamel figurals about $175. Not vermeil, but well finished.

### Cited Findings
- Support for each pick is cited in Questions 1–3: [1stDibs Corocraft sterling](https://www.1stdibs.com/buy/corocraft-sterling); [eBay Trifari sterling sold](https://ebay.com/itm/267070586957); [Kovels jelly belly](https://kovels.com/blog/costume-jewelry); [1stDibs Hattie Carnegie](https://www.1stdibs.com/creators/hattie-carnegie/fashion/brooches); [Trufaux Mazer](https://trufauxjewels.com/products/aquamarine-diamante-vermeil-sterling-flower-1940s-brooch-by-mazer); [1stDibs Reja](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/brooches/brooches/vintage-peacock-brooch-reja-sterling-silver-large-rhinestone-1940-usa/id-v_15793692); [Catawiki YSL](https://www.catawiki.com/en/l/101645842-yves-saint-laurent-metal-brooch); [1stDibs B&W](https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry/brooches/brooches/whimsical-frog-prince-figural-enamel-brooch-butler-wilson-1980s/id-v_12910612/)

### Inferences
- Boucher is out of budget for 1940s enamel birds. A later small Boucher gilt enamel figural, such as the c.1950s skunk-type pieces, could be a stretch pick if found at auction.
- Chanel, Boucher 1940s and Philippe jelly bellies should be presented as "aspirational, generally above CNY 2,000 landed".
- Under the stated budget, buying sterling vermeil and fine enamel and multiple rhinestones and a top-tier name all at once is rarely achievable. The buyer should expect to trade one of these off: maker prestige, size, or perfect condition.

### Gaps
- The ranking relies partly on retail asking prices and snippet-level eBay data. A focused eBay sold-listing pull (last 90 days) per pick would firm up the bands.
- Shipping, VAT and duty overhead assumptions await the logistics researcher.
